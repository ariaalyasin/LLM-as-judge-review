  \Large Paper 3:\\M-Prometheus: A Suite of Open Multilingual LLM Judges
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Introduction \& Category Alignment}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Category Context}: Research dedicated to creating standardized testing grounds to evaluate the evaluators themselves.
    \item \textbf{Core Objective}: Quantifying accuracy, consistency, and human-alignment of LLM judges across complex, non-English evaluation tasks.
    \item \textbf{Target Study}: Dissecting M-PROMETHEUS, a suite of open-weight multilingual LLM judges ranging from 3B to 14B parameters.
    \item \textbf{Practical Impact}: Bridging the severe automatic evaluation quality gap between English and non-English target languages.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Multilingual Evaluation Gap}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The English-Centric Bias}: Most high-performance LLM-as-a-judge frameworks are optimized exclusively for English text processing.
    \item \textbf{Systematic Disparity}: Lack of reliable non-English evaluators creates a bottleneck, directly hindering the development of robust multilingual models.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Flaws}: Prior open-source multi-lingual attempts fail to support complex pairwise comparisons or inherit brittle capabilities solely from base pretraining.
    \item \textbf{Automatic Metric Failure}: Traditional scalar metrics like BLEU or COMET fail to provide explanatory, text-based reasoning for qualitative decisions.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{M-PROMETHEUS Architecture \& Training Recipe}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model Foundation}: Built by fine-tuning state-of-the-art Qwen2.5-Instruct models across 3B, 7B, and 14B parameter scales.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Paradigm}: Completely bypasses translated text training data, instead leveraging native multilingual feedback synthesized from scratch.
    \item \textbf{Data Infusion Strategy}: Combines massive custom-generated direct assessment and pairwise preference collections with cross-lingual machine translation eval data.
    \item \textbf{Dual Capability Mode}: Explicitly trained to operate flexibly across both reference-based and reference-free evaluation scenarios.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Core Framework Strengths}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SOTA Multilingual Benchmarking}: Delivers unmatched evaluation performance against both open-weight and proprietary models in massive non-English ecosystems.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Task Knowledge Transfer}: Demonstrates that integrating machine translation data significantly boosts a judge's general ability to detect language hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{Compute Efficiency at Scale}: Smaller variants like the 3B model deliver massive performance gains relative to their size, making localized deployment practical.
    \item \textbf{Native Capability Preservation}: Drastically enhances multilingual capabilities without degrading core performance or reliability on English benchmarks.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Operational Limitations}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diminishing Returns on Scale}: Increasing parameter size to 14B yields structural improvements in theory, but lags behind smaller scales during active decoding tasks.
    \item \textbf{Language Saturation Constraints}: Directly optimized on only 6 language streams, relying heavily on the base model's zero-shot generalization for other target domains.
    \item \textbf{Explanatory Feedback Asymmetry}: While highly reliable at processing non-English prompts and inputs, the default long-form textual rationale remains written in English.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Critical Strategic Outlook}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Engineering Shift}: Establishes native synthetic multi-lingual generation as a fundamentally superior alternative to translating preexisting English datasets.
    \item \textbf{Active Model Development}: Transforms the judge from a passive post-hoc benchmarking script into an active, inference-time text-improvement engine.
    \item \textbf{Future Framework Evolution}: Future reliability paradigms must integrate localized non-English explanation chains and native multi-lingual reasoning paths.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Category 2: Bias Quantification \& Fairness Analysis}

\begin{frame}[standout]
  \hypertarget{paper5}{}
  \Large Paper 5:\\Any Large Language Model Can Be a Reliable Judge:Debiasing with a Reasoning-based Bias Detector
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Reliability Bottleneck: Systemic Pitfalls}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Category Context}: Isolating and quantifying systemic prejudices (position, verbosity, bandwagon, and sentiment biases) that undermine automated trust.
    \item \textbf{Four Structural Vulnerabilities Under Analysis}:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Verbosity Bias}: Prioritizing longer, low-quality answers over concise, mathematically accurate ones.
        \item \textbf{Position Bias}: Inherent preference for choices based on their order slot.
        \item \textbf{Bandwagon Bias}: Vulnerability to fabricated majority consensus indicators.
        \item \textbf{Sentiment Bias}: Distorting scores based on enthusiastic stylistic tone.
      \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Flaws of Existing Methods}: In-context prompting cannot break deep-seated cognitive patterns, while direct fine-tuning is impossible for closedendpoints.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Dataset Construction \& Distilled SFT Pipeline}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source Corpus Foundation}: Built upon curated samples from GSM8K (math), Arena (chat instruction pairs), and ScienceQA (multimodal science).
    \item \textbf{Data Curation Pipeline}:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Pairwise sets are modified to introduce targeted skews (e.g., stripping reasoning steps for verbosity, swapping choice order for position).
        \item Ground-truth bias labels are mathematically computed by comparing biased evaluations against control judgments.
      \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reasoning Distillation Training Loop}:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item A high-capacity Teacher LRM generates 1.67k expert reasoning traces explaining the flaws behind the judge's mistakes.
        \item Hard validation filtering retains only traces matching the ground-truth label.
        \item Base reasoning models (1.5B to 14B scales) undergo Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) jointly on all four bias types to learn core logical reasoning.
      \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Quantitative Evaluation \& Key Results}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Baseline Improvement}: Evaluated across 8 premier judges (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, DeepSeek-V3, and LLaMA-3.1 series).
    \item \textbf{Core Performance Metrics}:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Integrating RBD-8B boosts evaluation accuracy by an average of \textbf{18.5\%} and consistency by \textbf{10.9\%}.
        \item Outperforms prompt-based baselines by \textbf{12.8\%} and out-of-the-box fine-tuned judge models by \textbf{17.2\%}.
        \item Smaller engines see massive jumps; LLaMA-3.1-8B accuracy surges up to \textbf{71.8\%} on verbosity mitigation.
      \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stress Testing \& Scalability}:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Scaling the RBD from 1.5B to 14B steadily enhances average accuracy gains (+6.5\% to +15.4\%).
        \item Successfully mitigates multi-bias scenarios, securing recovery gains of +45\% and +63.8\% under overlapping vulnerabilities.
      \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Core Framework Strengths}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{True Cognitive Understanding}: Avoids exploiting synthetic data shortcuts by enforcing full paragraph-level comparative analysis before outputting labels.
    \item \textbf{Universal Agnostic Integration}: Operates entirely via text, facilitating seamless decoupling and deployment alongside proprietary, black-box commercial APIs.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Generalization}: Maintains peak performance on unseen benchmarks (LLMBar, JudgeBench) and new domains (FactQA) without task-specific training.
    \item \textbf{Negligible Resource Overhead}: Leveraging optimized inference backends achieves an ultra-low latency of 1.5 seconds per sample.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Operational Limitations}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cumulative Compute Cost}: While single-sample costs are fractional, multi-round iteration introduces cost aggregation when scaled across millions of requests.
    \item \textbf{Structural Constraint Scope}: Heavily tuned for multiple-choice and pairwise layouts; it does not natively adapt to purely open-ended generation score sheets.
    \item \textbf{Social Blind Spots}: Focuses strictly on surface-level format skews; it cannot explicitly target embedded demographic, cultural, or gender-based social biases.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Critical Strategic Outlook}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Scaled Stubbornness Trap}: Larger frontier models possess high internal certainty, making them structurally more stubborn and resistant to external correction.
    \item \textbf{The Authoritative Distillation Flaw}: Distilled, lightweight engines inherit the highly assertive tone of parents, masking an inability to deeply analyze foundational logic.
    \item \textbf{Future Paradigm Re-alignment}: Automated reliability cannot rely on internal alignment; trustworthy grading demands a hard transition toward decoupled, multi-agent referee structures.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Title \& Overview}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Paper}: \emph{Tuning LLM Judge Design Decisions for 1/1000 of the Cost}
    \item \textbf{Goal}: Replace expensive human evaluation with open-weight LLM judges --- no quality loss, $1/1000$ cost.
    \item \textbf{Category}: Scalability Limits \& Benchmark Robustness.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Problem Statement \& Current Challenges}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GPT-4 dependency}: sanctions risk, sudden behavior shifts, high cost.
    \item \textbf{Confounding factors}: model, prompt, hyperparameters change together (Alpaca-Eval, Arena-Hard).
    \item \textbf{Cognitive biases}: \emph{length}, \emph{position}, \emph{self-enhancement}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Scaling Laws \& Optimal Metric}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scaling up alone is not enough}: sub-7B judges act blindly, performing \emph{worse} than the length baseline.
    \item \textbf{Spearman Correlation} (whole-leaderboard vs.\ human):
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Noisy and costly; high coefficient of variation (CV) for small models.
        \item Grows only with many more instructions --- expensive to measure.
      \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Human Agreement} (chosen metric):
      \begin{itemize}
        \item High statistical stability, low noise ($CV \approx 1.3$).
        \item Stays \emph{stationary} across instruction counts.
        \item Distinguishes large models (32B, 72B) with far fewer, cheaper instructions.
      \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Takeaway}: Human Agreement exposes Alpaca-Eval's simplicity vs.\ Arena-Hard's robustness, at a fraction of the cost.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

    \item \textbf{Multi-Objective}: non-dominated sort $\rightarrow$ Pareto front (accuracy vs.\ token cost).

\begin{frame}{Critical Review}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Limited generalizability}: only 3 Decoder-only families; MoE unproven.
    \item \textbf{CoT rejection uncontrolled}: may be ``Lost in the Middle'' in sub-10B models, not judging per se.
    \item \textbf{Early-filtering risk}: Successive Halving assumes question homogeneity --- hard-task specialists may be wrongly early-stopped.
    \item \textbf{Goodhart's Law}: optimizing for human agreement amplifies hidden human biases.
    \item \textbf{Cost oversimplified}: ignores hardware setup, VRAM, orchestration for 32B/72B.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
